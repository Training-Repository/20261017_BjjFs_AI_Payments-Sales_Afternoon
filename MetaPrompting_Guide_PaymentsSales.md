## What Is Meta-Prompting?

Instead of writing a prompt yourself, you ask Copilot to write the prompt for you. You tell it your role, your task and who will read the output.

In effect, you are saying:

> *"You are a prompt expert. You know how to talk to AI. Write me a prompt I can use for my actual work."*

The output becomes your **reference prompt**. You refine it once and reuse it every day: for a merchant pitch, a beat plan, an objection, a follow-up message or a team review.

> 📱 **In the field?** Everything in this guide works in the **Microsoft 365 Copilot app** or **Teams** on your phone. Save your best prompts in a note on your phone so you can paste them between visits.

---

## The Core Meta-Prompt Formula

```
Act as a Microsoft Copilot prompt expert.
I am a [YOUR ROLE] at [COMPANY/CONTEXT].
I need to [WHAT YOU WANT TO DO].
My audience is [WHO WILL READ THE OUTPUT].
Write me a detailed, structured Copilot prompt I can use to complete this task.
The prompt should specify: role, task, context, constraints, output format, and tone.
```

## Meta-Prompts for Payments Field Sales — Bajaj Finance

Copy, paste and run each of these in Copilot. Then use the prompt it writes as your working prompt.

### 1. Merchant Pitch by Category

```
Act as a Microsoft Copilot prompt expert.
I am an FOS (Feet on Street) sales executive in the Payments team at Bajaj Finance Limited. I visit shops —
kirana stores, pharmacies, restaurants, apparel, jewellers, salons, clinics — and sign them up for
UPI QR, a QR with a voice-alert soundbox, an Android POS machine, or payment links. I have about
two minutes with the owner, who is busy serving customers.

My audience is the shop owner.

Write me a Copilot prompt I can use to prepare a short pitch for any merchant category. The prompt
should specify: the merchant category and size I will provide, the product to recommend and why,
three benefits in the owner's language (not technical), one likely objection with a reply, and a
closing line. Tone: simple, respectful, confident. Keep it under 120 words so I can say it aloud.
```

---

### 2. Daily Beat Plan

```
Act as a Microsoft Copilot prompt expert.
I am an FOS (Feet on Street) sales executive in the Payments team at Bajaj Finance Limited. Each morning I plan my visits from
my onboarding pipeline: new leads, merchants with KYC pending, devices to install, and merchants who
have a device but have not started transacting. Activation within 7 days of install is my key target.

My audience is me, and my Team Lead who reviews my plan.

Write me a Copilot prompt that turns my pipeline into a sequenced visit plan for the day. The prompt
should tell Copilot how to prioritise (inactive merchants close to 7 days first, then installs due,
then KYC pending, then new leads), group visits by area to cut travel, give one objective per visit,
and present it as a short numbered list I can read on my phone.
```

---

### 3. Objection Handling

```
Act as a Microsoft Copilot prompt expert.
I am an FOS (Feet on Street) sales executive in the Payments team at Bajaj Finance Limited. Merchants often raise objections:
"customers pay cash", "the soundbox rental is too high", "settlement takes too long", "I already
use another QR", "are there charges on UPI?". I have our offer sheet with prices, waivers and
settlement terms.

My audience is the merchant, face to face.

Write me a Copilot prompt that gives me a reply to any objection, using only the facts in our offer
sheet. The prompt should specify: acknowledge the concern, answer in two or three short sentences
with one fact from the sheet, ask a question that moves the conversation forward, and never promise
anything that is not in the sheet. Tone: calm, honest, non-pushy.
```

---

### 4. Activation Follow-up Message

```
Act as a Microsoft Copilot prompt expert.
I am an FOS (Feet on Street) sales executive in the Payments team at Bajaj Finance Limited. Some merchants have a QR, soundbox
or POS installed but have not taken a single payment yet. I want to send them a short WhatsApp
message before my revisit, in English and in Hindi or Marathi.

My audience is the merchant.

Write me a Copilot prompt I can reuse for these messages. The prompt should specify: the merchant
name, product and the concern they raised; a friendly reminder of one benefit; a simple step to take
the first payment today; the time of my revisit; and my name. Under 60 words per language. Tone:
warm, helpful, not salesy. No links unless I give one.
```

---

### 5. Team Performance Review

```
Act as a Microsoft Copilot prompt expert.
I am a Team Lead in Payments sales at Bajaj Finance Limited. Each month I review my FOS executives
on onboarding against target, activation within 7 days (target 80%), new payment value (TPV) and
field visits, for the last three months.

My audience is my Area Manager in our monthly review.

Write me a structured Copilot prompt that will produce this review from my performance sheet. The
prompt should specify the format (3-line summary + table by FOS + who needs support and why + two
actions for next month), the tone (factual, fair, constructive), and what to surface (activation
below 80%, a falling trend, a gap between onboarding and activation).
```

### 6. Visit Notes to CRM Entry

```
Act as a Microsoft Copilot prompt expert.
I am an FOS (Feet on Street) sales executive in the Payments team at Bajaj Finance Limited. After each visit I type or dictate
rough notes on my phone. I need them turned into a clean visit record for our CRM.

My audience is my Team Lead and the CRM.

Write me a Copilot prompt that converts rough notes into a record with these fields: merchant,
purpose of visit, outcome, objection raised, next action, follow-up date. It should not add anything
I did not say, and should mark any missing field as "not captured". Tone: factual, short.
```

## Still Not Right? Run This.

If the prompt Copilot wrote doesn't feel right, don't rewrite it yourself. Use this follow-up:

```
The prompt you wrote is a good start, but I need you to adjust it.
Here is what I want to change:

- [What felt off — e.g., "the pitch is too long to say in two minutes"]
- [What's missing — e.g., "it doesn't mention the rental waiver"]
- [The tone issue — e.g., "it sounds too formal for a kirana owner"]

Please rewrite the prompt with these corrections.
Keep the same structure (Role / Task / Context / Format / Tone) but fix the parts I've described.
```

## What to Do With the Output

1. **Read it.** Does it sound like your actual day in the field?
2. **Replace the placeholders** with a real category, product, objection or area.
3. **Run it** on the exercise data from this session.
4. **Save it** in a note on your phone. It is now your reusable prompt.
